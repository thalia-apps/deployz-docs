## Read the recovery panel

When a deployment fails or needs attention, its page in the dashboard opens with a recovery panel. Read it from top to bottom:

1. **What happened.** The heading names the failure: **Install failed**, **Application couldn't start**, **Update failed**, **Rollback failed**, **Restart failed**, **Configuration update failed**, or **Removal failed**. The sentence below it says what happened in plain language. **Application couldn't start** covers the six application startup codes: `CONTAINER_START_FAILED`, `IMAGE_HEALTH_CHECK_FAILED`, `PORT_MISMATCH`, `MISSING_SECRET`, `MIGRATION_FAILED`, and `DATABASE_CONNECTION_FAILED`.
2. **Impact.** What still works. After a failed update, for example, the panel says that the previous release is still live and unaffected.
3. **Cause and fix.** **Why it happened** and **How to fix it**. For the six application startup codes and for `UNKNOWN`, Deployz can write the explanation from the failure signals instead. It then shows **Likely cause** and **Suggested fix**, and may say how sure it is. Check that kind of explanation against **Technical details** before you act on it.
4. **Recoverability.** One line that says whether the failure can clear on its own or who needs to act. The classes are below.
5. **Actions.** The recovery action, for example **Retry deployment**, **Retry update**, or **Retry removal**. When a failed first install needs Deployz, the panel shows the text **Contact Deployz support** instead of a button.
6. **Technical details.** Collapsed by default. It holds the **Failure code**, the operation and attempt, the AWS resource that failed, error messages, and, for startup failures, the container's exit code and stopped reason.

Your customer sees a simpler panel on their install page. It explains what happened, whether anything in their AWS account was cleaned up, and whether they need to act. It doesn't show the failure code.

## Who needs to act

Every failure code belongs to one recoverability class. The class tells you who needs to act, and whether retrying can help. For each code's class, see [Failure codes](/reference/failure-codes). For how to retry, see [How to retry](/reference/failure-codes#how-to-retry).

| Class | Line in the recovery panel | Who acts | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Can be temporary | “This can be temporary. Retry when the cause clears. If it continues, contact Deployz support.” | You, once the cause clears | On a failed first install, select **Retry deployment**. A failed first install doesn't recover on its own. On a deployment with a running release, the deployment returns to its normal status when the connector reports the application healthy again, and you can also deploy again. If it continues, [contact support](https://share.hsforms.com/1dOsn_DJtRhq47DXIaRjeLw5auur). |
| Needs a change | “Needs a change before a retry can succeed — see the fix above.” | You, or your customer in their AWS account | Make the change the fix describes, then retry. |
| Needs Deployz | “This needs a fix on the Deployz side — contact support rather than retrying.” | Deployz | [Contact support](https://share.hsforms.com/1dOsn_DJtRhq47DXIaRjeLw5auur). Don't retry in a loop. On a failed first install, the dashboard shows the text **Contact Deployz support** instead of **Retry deployment**. |
| Can't be retried as is | “Retrying will not help until the underlying requirement changes.” | You | Change the requirement, for example the Region or the application itself. |

Your customer can't retry, update, or remove anything in Deployz. When the fix is in their AWS account, they make the change and tell you, and you retry.

## Contact support

[Contact support](https://share.hsforms.com/1dOsn_DJtRhq47DXIaRjeLw5auur) when:

- The recovery panel says the failure needs a fix on the Deployz side.
- You followed the fix for a failure code and the failure keeps happening.
- The failure code is `UNKNOWN` and **Technical details** doesn't explain it.

Include:

- The **Deployment ID** and the **Failure code**, from **Technical details** on the deployment page. Include the **Failure reference** if it's shown.
- The application name and the customer.
- What you were doing, and roughly when it failed, with the time zone.
- What you already tried.

Don't send passwords, secret values, or your customer's private data.
